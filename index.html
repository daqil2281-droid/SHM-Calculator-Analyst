Simple pendulum: SHM simulation

For small angles, a simple pendulum approximates simple harmonic motion. Amplitude below is the maximum horizontal displacement from equilibrium, xₘₐₓ = L sin θ₀. Instantaneous displacement is x = L sin θ, where θ changes during the swing.

Heat dissipation: oscillating vs static pendulum

This is an illustrative energy-to-temperature model, not a direct laboratory measurement. A moving pendulum loses mechanical energy through air resistance and pivot friction; that dissipated energy can warm the bob and surroundings slightly. A static bob has no oscillation energy to dissipate, so its temperature approaches ambient mainly through ordinary cooling.

Illustrative model parameters are fixed for fair comparison.

How to use

  1. Enter pendulum length l in metres.
  2. Enter the three measured times for 20 oscillations.
  3. The app calculates taverage, T and T² automatically.
  4. Use the graph to inspect the linear relationship between T² and l.
  5. Read the gradient, R² and calculated g.

Physics relation: T = 2π√(l/g), therefore T² = (4π²/g)l and g = 4π²/gradient.
